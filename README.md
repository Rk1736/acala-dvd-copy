## How It Works

Under the hood, Acala DVD Copy treats a disc the way a careful archivist treats a manuscript — reading every track, every menu, every subtitle stream, then rebuilding it pixel-for-pixel in a fresh container. The engine scans the source disc, maps its structure, and assembles a working image that mirrors the original layout, so menus still click and chapters still land where they should.

When a disc is too large for its destination, the compression stage steps in. Instead of blunt cropping, it analyzes the video bitrate scene by scene and reallocates space where it matters most — quiet dialogue scenes shrink slightly, high-motion sequences keep their detail. The result is a copy that fits a standard disc while staying visually faithful to the source.

The interface keeps the workflow visual: pick a source, preview the disc structure as a tree of titles and tracks, choose a target size, and watch the progress bar sweep across as the copy is built. Everything happens locally on your Windows machine — no cloud round-trips, no waiting on uploads. From rip to burn-ready image, the whole pipeline runs in one window, so you can queue up a stack of discs and let the utility work through them while you do something else.

## Workflow

From disc to digital shelf in four visual beats: load, shape, preview, and burn. Acala DVD Copy keeps each step on one screen so you always know exactly what is happening to your movie.

**1. Load the disc.** Slide your DVD into the drive and point Acala DVD Copy at it. The utility reads the disc structure and lays out every title, track, and audio stream as a clear checklist — nothing hidden, nothing guessed. Pick the main feature or cherry-pick the extras you want to keep.

**2. Shape the copy.** This is where the toolkit earns its name. Choose an exact duplicate for a faithful clone, or let the compression engine squeeze the content down until it fits a standard disc. Adjust target size, trim menus or subtitles, and reorder titles with simple drags. The size meter updates as you work, so you can see the fit before you commit.

**3. Preview the result.** Run a quick preview pass to check chapter markers, audio selection, and compression quality. If something looks off, step back and reshape — the workflow never locks you in until you press the final button.

**4. Burn or export.** Send the finished project straight to a blank disc, or save it as a disc image for archiving. A progress view tracks the write in real time, and a completion summary confirms the copy is ready to shelve.

The rhythm stays the same whether you are preserving one treasured disc or working through a whole collection: load, shape, preview, burn. Repeat until the shelf is safe.

## FAQ

**Will the copy look identical to my original disc?**
Pixel for pixel, yes—the exact-copy mode replicates the disc structure without re-encoding, so what you see on your shelf is what you see on the copy.

**My disc is larger than a standard DVD. Can it still fit?**
That's the compression engine's job. Point it at a standard target size and it rebuilds the video at a lower bitrate, balancing sharpness and file size so the whole feature lands on one disc.

**How long does a typical copy take?**
Exact copies move at drive speed and finish quickly; compressed copies take longer because the encoder is doing the heavy lifting. Set your batch queue before you step away and let it run.

**Can I queue several discs at once?**
Yes. Load the drive, build your queue, and the batch workflow handles them one after another—ideal for archiving a season box set over a weekend.

**Will the copy play in my living-room player?**
The output is built for standard disc playback, not just computer drives. Keep your target set to a standard DVD profile and the resulting disc should behave like the original.

**What happens to the menus and extras?**
The copy pipeline carries over the disc's structure—menus, chapter stops, bonus material—so navigation feels the same. If space is tight, compression prioritizes the main feature.

**Does it touch my original disc?**
Only to read it. The source disc is never written to, so your originals stay untouched while the copy is assembled.

**How do I get the best picture quality?**
Start with an exact copy whenever the disc fits. When compression is unavoidable, choose the highest quality preset your target size allows and let the encoder do a single careful pass.

**Where do my finished copies go?**
You choose the output folder before the run begins—keep a dedicated archive folder so every disc lands in the same tidy place.

**Something looks off in the finished copy. Where do I start?**
Re-run the job at a gentler compression setting first; if the issue persists, verify the source disc reads cleanly. A scratchy original is the usual culprit, not the tool.
